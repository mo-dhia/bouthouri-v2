# Shared route surface

The locale layout renders an initially transparent, noninteractive fixed surface.
Its null gate reserves a direct editorial arrival before optional page effects,
then dynamically imports the controller. A failed import consumes that reservation
and exposes readable page state. No graphics, models or audio context are booted
by this boundary.

Body capture runs after HOME document capture and before React Link bubble. Both
ordinary anchors and Links use one cover/push/reveal path. Unsupported routes,
Projects, hashes, modifiers, downloads and effective new-window targets remain
unclaimed. Covered signals address the exact outgoing Header DOM owner, which
releases its menu lock without opener focus before navigation or scroll writes.

Only opacity has a finite surface RAF, using source sine in/out and the declared
desktop/tablet/mobile and reduced durations. Page entry remains on each page's
existing RAF. Readiness uses retained publisher callbacks, DOM/size observers,
fonts and bounded160/4500/8000ms timers; there is no waiting ticker. A visible
practice canvas must publish its successful first draw or depth fallback. Offscreen
and reduced arrivals do not wait for that canvas;4500ms bounds asset failure. The
root and inner page must match the server route
identity; late/disconnected publishers cannot complete a different destination.

The entry registry retains consumed decisions per physical owner and path. A
missing/failed enhancement or timed-out arrival becomes readable; a late import
adopts the completed pose. Same-owner optional-motion remounts do not replay entry.
Held headings remain in their readable pose until release under full cover; the
tiny gate separately bounds a never-settling controller import at4500ms.
History and reduced arrivals skip entry, using one width-scaled80-reference-pixel
threshold elsewhere. Contact settles completion through its own finite clock.

Editorial entries get their own identity and boundary-only pixels, with a bounded
session map and an inline checkpoint fallback. HOME identities and persistence
remain owned by HOME. A validated same-locale editorial HOME push clears generic
and work returns and establishes top before hero selection. Only a real selected
same-locale editorial→HOME history transaction may provide explicit router pixels;
HOME locale transitions retain the accepted semantic policy. History never focuses.
The cached source head, rather than RouteSurface, sets global manual restoration.
This controller preserves the accepted HOME policy and does not install a blanket
manual flag. It samples only the outgoing native input number in memory, validating
entry identity before boundary persistence, so selected destination pixels cannot
overwrite an outgoing checkpoint. Same-path/hash traversal retains its native path.

Pointer/focus intent and a claimed soft click call Next prefetch with40-entry
deduplication, including plain anchors. There is no model/GPU warmup or hard-locale
prefetch through this boundary.

Each managed transition retains its originating URL/entry and its selected target
entry. A pending push may keep the origin selected until its own router commit;
another route/entry cancels the hold, opacity work and watchdog. Ordinary unclaimed
same-window internal activation cancels before Next/native handling without being
prevented. Other claimed managed links remain consumed while the first transition
owns navigation. Selection is rechecked at finite animation callbacks, destination
updates, completion and existing timeout boundaries, without a new polling clock.

Boundary capture reads live native pixels only for the exact selected path/search,
entry ID and current main/inner DOM owners. A departed connected owner keeps its
already observed pixels; pagehide cannot replace them with a shortened incoming
document's offset. The passive memory sampler also rejects a replacement owner.

An uncommitted push may have selected another URL while its original physical
owner remains connected. A checkpoint-valid editorial Back to that exact origin
URL takes history restoration despite the matching physical pathname. A different
known selected entry uses its own checkpoint. Hash/query/native unknown-entry and
unchanged-selection controls stay native; HOME keeps its existing owner policy.
Pending editorial restore pixels are retained until application rather than being
replaced by an incoming clamp. No additional frame owner or timer is introduced.

History retention follows the previous logical selection across an uncommitted
history Forward as well as a push. Pagehide retains an unfinished editorial target,
exact entry selection, elapsed active time and reveal progress. Persisted pageshow
resumes only that still-selected intent through the existing bounded mechanism;
superseding entries, hashes and routes discard it. Completed BFCache visits do not
replay arrival, and HOME retains its separate restore owner.

Owner reconciliation also runs during reveal. A replacement's publisher is held
until transfer to the current main/inner owner; an already-started arrival transfers
its skip decision. Replacement invalidates only its physical application and stale
completion callbacks. The surface pauses and continues the remaining source-eased
reveal progress, without restarting its duration, while the retained history target
is applied to the new ready owner. Reset/disposal settles canceled finite work and
held candidates. No readiness ticker, additional RAF owner or retry timer is added.

Saved-editorial restoration is identified by a finite nonnegative validated target,
including zero, on an implemented non-HOME route. The same predicate protects
pending capture/input, initial and replacement adoption, application and suspension
for both direct reload/back_forward and popstate arrivals. No-saved fresh/native
arrivals are excluded. A target is retained even before the first async owner update.

Suspension also retains the original transaction kind, entry-skip decision, focus
permission and focus origin. Resuming a direct saved arrival remains direct: its
remaining160ms minimum, reload/back_forward focus distinction, remaining active
deadlines and finite reveal progress survive. Completed visits do not replay, and
an exact URL/entry/native-key mismatch discards the suspended target.

Pagehide cancels finite jobs; BFCache resumes without replaying direct entry.
Cleanup restores owned opacity, removes observers/listeners/timers and cancels
entry completion. Server copy and native navigation stay available without the
controller. See `docs/editorial-route-handoff-source-contract.md` for provenance
and intentional differences from source.

Skipping the entry animation is independent of asset readiness. A visible
practice hero waits for its first static presentation or graphics failure even
when reduced/history entry skips the animated entrance. The registry retains
an explicit readable-fallback decision separately from `skip`; registering a
new handler clears that fallback without replaying completed entry. Offscreen
eligibility and the bounded asset ceiling remain. This follows source `D`'s
assets predicate, which has no reduced-mode shortcut; it adds no polling RAF.

## Route callback clock

R4 executed the cached source core32919's fresh `gsap.to` route tween with a
controlled Date clock and actual internal ticker callbacks. Source samples the
callback clock, rather than the browser's supplied refresh timestamp. Delayed
dispatch makes those clocks differ even within the same finite animation.
The local route keeps its synchronous `tick(start)` and existing segment start,
duration, sine easing and resume phase. Subsequent callbacks sample
`win.performance.now()` before calling that same tick. No RAF, global ticker,
parent clock or duration constant was added.

The mounted regression retains source opacity values at 80/96ms while supplied
refresh stamps lag by 46.42/13.08ms. It failed before the sampling change and
passes afterward. This corrects the sample-clock contract; it does not recreate
GSAP's global parent-start phase or its default long-stall smoothing. R4's
controlled delayed schedule is not a recovered browser writer trace. R3's
reduced-mode visible/wall-time observation remains separate and requires fresh
production adjudication; no missed frames or performance improvement is claimed.

The corrected integration passed 106 focused route/HOME tests and the full
364-test check with 15,615 assertions, lint, types and architecture. Build
`iTxQ9za0P2IZUlsc_NBns` and the HTTP SSR/20-asset audit also passed. Logs and
the two-input change manifest are in the root `route-clock-r5` artifacts.
Fresh independent production timing/transition review remains pending.
